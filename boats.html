Boats, hatches & lockers

Stop mildew in boats before it starts

Boat mildew thrives in closed, damp hatches and lockers. A Stop Mildew pouch keeps rope, sail and hatch spaces fresh for 3–6 months.

Why boats grow mildew

A boat is a small, closed-up space that is often shut for weeks at a time. Add damp sea air and you have the perfect setting for mildew. Hatches, rope lockers and sail lockers are the usual trouble spots. Canvas, rope, cushions and clothing all take on that musty smell.

Stop Mildew is a 92% paraformaldehyde mildew inhibitor. Its vapour stops the damaging mould growth that causes mildew. It is made for places that are closed and damp, which describes most boats between trips. It is also ideal for humid coastal homes and anything stored away for the season.

How to use Stop Mildew in boat hatches and lockers

Use 28 g for every 2.9 m³ of closed storage space. Here is a worked example. A rope locker measuring 2.5 m × 1.5 m × 1.5 m holds about 5.6 m³. That is just under 5.8 m³, so two pouches cover it. A small hatch locker well under 2.9 m³ needs one pouch.

  • Place the pouch inside the hatch, rope locker or sail locker.
  • Keep the hatch or locker lid shut. The pouch only works in a closed space.
  • One pouch lasts 3–6 months.
  • When the pouch is empty, swap in a fresh one.

Where on the boat

  • Boat hatches and storage lockers.
  • Rope and sail lockers.
  • Fishing bags and tackle boxes. See the FAQ for more questions.

Things to watch

  • Anything you store must be completely dry before it is exposed to Stop Mildew. Dry sails, rope and clothing before you pack them away.
  • Before working in an enclosed space, remove the pouch and keep the hatch open for several hours to air it.
  • Stop Mildew is not for storing film or articles made of plastic. Check that before you put a pouch in a locker with plastic items.
  • Never place a pouch in refrigerators or food containers. That includes cool boxes used for food.

Guidance for marine electronics, cabin cushions and specific sail fabrics

Marine electronics

Don’t put pouches inside enclosed spaces with electronics, such as chart table cabinets, instrument panels or switchboard lockers. Stop Mildew is not suitable for electronics.

Cabin cushions: only when they’re stored away

  • Fabric cushions (acrylic, polyester or cotton covers) stored off-season in a closed locker are fine, as long as they’re completely dry and aired out before use.
  • Vinyl or plastic-covered cushions fall under our “not for plastic articles” warning.
  • Not in the cabin itself. The pouch is meant for closed storage, not spaces where people sit or sleep.

Sails: it depends on the cloth

  • Polyester (Dacron) sails stored dry in a closed sail locker are fine.
  • Laminate sails have a plastic film layer, often Mylar, which our “not for film or plastic articles” warning covers. Check with the sailmaker.
  • Nylon spinnakers, and Kevlar or carbon sails: ask the sailmaker or cloth manufacturer, or test a sample first.
  • Wet sails: sails must be completely dry first, as with garments. Packing a wet sail with a pouch won’t stop mildew and goes against the instructions.
