Why books and papers go mouldy

Paper soaks up damp from the air. In a humid coastal home, a closed cupboard or a glassed-in bookcase can hold that damp for months. The result is mould on books, spotted pages, curled covers and that stale, musty smell.

Stop Mildew is a 92% paraformaldehyde mildew inhibitor. Its vapour stops the damaging mould growth that causes mildew. It protects books, paper and documents from mould, mildew and musty, stale odours. It also suits cellars and attics, where boxes of papers are often kept.

How to use Stop Mildew in a bookcase or document store

Use 28 g (one pouch) for every 2.9 m³ of closed storage space. Here is a worked example. A glassed-in bookcase measuring 2 m × 0.3 m × 2.1 m holds about 1.3 m³, so one pouch is enough. A cellar store of 5.8 m³ would need two pouches.

  • Place the pouch inside the bookcase, trunk or document box.
  • Keep the bookcase doors and box lids shut. The pouch only works in a closed space.
  • One pouch lasts 3–6 months.
  • When the pouch is empty, swap in a fresh one.

Where to use it

  • Glassed-in bookcases.
  • Trunks and chests holding papers and files.
  • Cellars and attics used for storage.
  • Wardrobes and closets. See mildew in wardrobes.

Things to watch

  • Books and papers must be completely dry before they are exposed to Stop Mildew. Air damp books first.
  • Before working in the bookcase, remove the pouch and keep the doors open for several hours to air it.
  • Not for storing film or articles made of plastic. That includes photographic film and plastic sleeves.
  • Never place a pouch in refrigerators or food containers.
Owner to confirm: advice for rare, leather-bound or archival material.
